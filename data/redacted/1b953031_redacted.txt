1. Introduction
1.1 Purpose
The purpose of this document is to assist Cadence in understanding and operationalizing the enhancements made to its Third-Party Risk Management (TPRM) capabilities through the implementation of the OneTrust TPRM solution. This engagement aims to provide Cadence with a standardized, scalable, and automated solution that aligns with its business, operational, and compliance requirements. l 
As part of this engagement, [PERSON_1] supported the implementation and configuration of Cadence’s OneTrust TPRM instance. This comprises:
Designing and implementing structured workflows aligned with Cadence’s operational needs and [PERSON_2] requirements.
Configuring user roles to ensure appropriate access management.
Establishing vendor onboarding attributes to streamline supplier risk processes.
Refreshing and tailoring the Due Diligence Questionnaire to ensure alignment with Cadence’s risk assessment objectives and regulatory requirements.
Through this effort, Cadence will attain a more structured, transparent, and efficient [PERSON_2] framework, reducing manual effort and ensuring compliance with internal and external risk requirements.
1.2 Scope
The scope of this document focuses on Cadence’s TPRM processes, and the responsibilities of internal stakeholders involved in managing third-party risks. The following activities are included within this scope:
Current State Analysis: Review the current state of Cadence’s OneTrust TPRM instance, including its configuration, workflows, and supporting processes, while also assessing alignment with the broader [PERSON_2] program.
Refreshing the Due Diligence Questionnaire: Enhance and update existing questionnaires to align with Cadence’s organizational structure, compliance needs, and industry best practices.
Configuration of OneTrust TPRM: Document and configure custom workflows, rules, questionnaire templates, and notification processes in OneTrust tailored to Cadence’s requirements.
Training & Knowledge Transfer: Provide documentation covering OneTrust setup and configuration, workflow logics and questionnaire rules, ensuring Cadence stakeholders can effectively manage and operate the solution.

3. Transition Toward Integrated Risk Management

Based on: OneTrust TPRM Training
The visual above, adapted from OneTrust’s framework, illustrates the maturity progression from compliance-driven and siloed risk functions toward integrated and business-enabling risk management.
Currently, [PERSON_5] program is positioned at the “Siloed Risk Function” stage, where risk ownership and assessment activities are distributed across functions such as Legal, Compliance, Security, and Procurement. Each area maintains its own risk processes, resulting in fragmented oversight.
The strategic objective of this engagement is to transition Cadence toward the “Integrated Risk Management (IRM)” stage - where all third-party risks are collectively managed through a unified, automated, and standardized framework within OneTrust. This will establish a single source of truth for vendor risk visibility, governance, and decision-making.
A key enabler of this transformation is the integration between Ironclad and OneTrust, which connects contract lifecycle management with third-party risk workflows. Through this integration:
Contract creation and execution in Ironclad automatically triggers corresponding due diligence and assessment workflows in OneTrust.
Vendor-related data (e.g., contract terms, renewal dates, risk tier, and compliance status) synchronizes between systems for continuous visibility.
Risk and contractual obligations are managed cohesively within a single ecosystem.

Optiv’s implementation approach supports this evolution by consolidating all risk-related processes into OneTrust and aligning them with two primary risk domains:
Operational Risks:
Identified through due diligence questionnaires covering Legal, Financial, Privacy, and Compliance areas. These assessments capture vendor-level dependencies, regulatory exposures, and process inefficiencies at both organizational and product/service levels.
Cyber Risks:
Identified through security questionnaires focusing on data protection, access control, incident management, and infrastructure security.
By structuring risk assessments around these two domains, and integrating [PERSON_6] with OneTrust, Cadence can now:
Manage operational and cybersecurity risks in a unified workstream.
Automate due diligence, escalation, and remediation workflows.
Maintain cross-functional visibility across all risk stakeholders.
Strengthen accountability through consistent, platform-driven governance.
Progress toward a mature Integrated Risk Management model that aligns risk oversight with Cadence’s business objectives and growth strategy.

4.2. Workflow for Third-Party types
Cadence’s third-party workflows in OneTrust are now fully automated and tailored to the criticality and type of vendor.
Third parties are classified based on data sensitivity and risk criticality, with separate workflows established for Tier 1, [PERSON_7], and Tier 3 vendors. Tier 3 third parties typically represent lower-risk engagements (e.g., limited data access or short-term service contracts), while Tiers 1 and 2 represent medium- and high-risk third parties requiring enhanced due diligence.
Additionally, classification by third-party Category ensures that distinct workflows are automatically triggered for Staffing Vendors, Service Providers, Marketing Partners, Revenue Partners, and International Vendors.
When a new third-party is added to the OneTrust platform, it is routed through an automated onboarding workflow that analyzes both third-party tier and category. Based on these attributes, the system assigns the appropriate workflow path for due diligence, assessment, and approval.
4.2.1 Onboarding Workflow
Cadence’s OneTrust environment has been configured with automated workflows that manage the lifecycle of third-party assessments based on both vendor category and risk tier. The integration between Ironclad and OneTrust ensures that every new vendor entering the system is automatically analyzed and assigned to the appropriate workflow.


4.2.2 Tier 1 and 2 Workflows
Vendors tiered under Tier 1 and Tier 2 undergo a comprehensive due diligence process, differentiated by data criticality and risk profile. The following vendor categories fall under this group: Staffing Vendors, Service Providers and Vendors, Marketing Partners, Revenue Partners, and International Vendors. Each category is assigned a tailored due diligence questionnaire, which is automatically sent to the Primary Vendor Contact upon workflow initiation.

6.3.2 Questionnaire Template Rules
Automation rules are created within questionnaire templates to analyze responses provided by the Primary Vendor Contact (PVC). These rules automatically generate risk records based on the vendor’s answers and predefined logic.
To create a questionnaire rule:
Open “Third-Party Risk Management” in OneTrust.
[PERSON_11] to the “Templates” section.
Select the questionnaire in which rules are to be added.
Ensure all questions are created and saved before rule configuration.
Click on the “Rules” tab.
Click “Add Rule” to create a new logic condition.


To define the logic for a rule, perform the following steps:
Add “Rule Name”.
Select “Trigger” as Question.
Choose the relevant question from the list.
Set “Operator” as Equal To.
Choose Yes, No, or any other applicable option based on the question design.
Select “Action” as “Create risk”.



Action dropdown within the rule setup offers multiple options that can be used to generate risks, create tasks, or trigger follow-up actions.


Currently, the configuration focuses on the “Create risk” action type. When defining a rule, OneTrust provides two options for risk creation:
Create risk from the library
Create new risk
To use a risk from the library, users can select an existing predefined risk. If a new risk needs to be created, follow the process below:
Select “Create new risk”.
Assign a “Risk category”.
Define scoring parameters.
Add “Risk name” and “Description”.
Assign “Deadline” and “Reminder frequency” as per SLA.
Identify responsible stakeholders or approvers.
Determine whether a “Control” is to be added.
If no control is required, click “Save” to finalize the rule.





Adding Risk controls: Determining the correct risk scoring and associated controls is critical, as it influences the mitigation timeline and monitoring cadence. To add a control for a documented risk, follow the steps below:
Click “Add risk control”.
Choose whether to add a control from the “Control library” or to create a Custom control.
For custom controls, select “Create custom control” and complete the required fields.
Add control details including the control name, category, and implementation guidance.
Click “Save” once the control information is finalized.





The control becomes linked to the risk within OneTrust and is displayed whenever the risk is triggered during an assessment. This ensures consistent tracking and documentation of mitigation measures across third parties and assessments.

Workflow Customization:

Once stages are defined, additional automation rules and triggers are added within the workflow tabs. These rules control actions such as:
Sending notifications.
Launching due diligence questionnaires.
Advancing workflow stages automatically based on responses.
Changing workflow paths (for reassessments or renewals).
This stage-wise automation ensures smooth progression of vendors through their respective assessment processes.
Workflow Dependencies and Publishing:
Before adding rules to the onboarding workflow, all other workflows (for Tier 1, [PERSON_7], and Tier 3 third parties) must be developed and finalized. This is because the onboarding workflow relies on these workflows for routing vendors to the correct due diligence stream.
Therefore, it is essential to first complete and publish the Tier-specific workflows before assigning them to the onboarding workflow configuration.
Once all workflows are created and validated, they can be viewed in the Vendor Workflows list.


Note: A workflow should only be published after all rules, triggers, and automation logic have been configured and validated. Publishing prematurely may lead to incomplete routing or broken dependencies within the automation.
