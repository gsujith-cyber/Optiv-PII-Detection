1. Purpose and objectives

Cadence Financial Group, Inc. (the "“Group") accepts risk as an inherent consequence of providing investment banking, asset
management and consumer banking services. This policy establishes the Group-wide framework through which risk is identified,
assessed, treated, monitored and reported, so that risk-taking is deliberate, understood, and remains within the appetite set by the
Board of Directors.

This policy is the apex document of the Group risk policy hierarchy. All subordinate risk policies, standards and procedures must be
consistent with it. Where a subordinate document conflicts with this policy, this policy prevails. Interpretation questions are directed to
the policy author, [PERSON_2] ([EMAIL_2], +44 20 7555 0171).

1.1 Objectives

1. Protect capital, liquidity and earnings by ensuring exposures remain within approved appetite and regulatory limits.
2. Enable informed risk-taking through a consistent, comparable view of risk across business lines and geographies.
3. Establish clear accountability for every material risk, with a single named owner at an appropriate level of seniority.
4, Ensure regulatory compliance with prudential, conduct and data-protection obligations in each jurisdiction.

5. Protect customers and market integrity by embedding conduct considerations into risk decisions.

6. Safeguard reputation by identifying and managing risks to stakeholder confidence.

7. Support resilience so the Group can absorb severe but plausible shocks and continue important business services.
8. Promote a healthy risk culture in which concerns are raised early, escalated without fear, and acted upon.

1.2 Principles

PRINCIPLE STATEMENT

Risk ownership Risk is owned by the business that creates it. Ownership cannot be delegated to a control function or to a third party.
Proportionality The depth of assessment, control and reporting is proportionate to the materiality and complexity of the risk.
Independence The second and third lines have direct, unrestricted access to the Board and are free from business pressure.
Transparency Risks are reported completely and without filtering. Understating or delaying a risk report is a breach of this policy.
Forward focus The framework considers emerging and prospective risks, not only realised losses and historical events.
[PERSON_8] taxonomy, scoring scale and definitions apply Group-wide so that risks aggregate meaningfully.
Substance over form Assessments reflect actual exposure and actual control operation, not documented intent.

3.3 Relationship to the internal control framework

Risk assessment determines what could go wrong and how severely; the control framework determines what is done to prevent or
detect it. The two are linked through the control library, in which every control maps to at least one risk in the Group Risk Register, and
every risk rated [PERSON_20] or above maps to at least one control.

3.4 Relationship to capital and liquidity planning

The output of risk identification and assessment is a direct input to the Internal Capital Adequacy Assessment Process (ICAAP) and the
Internal Liquidity Adequacy Assessment Process (ILAAP). Material risks not adequately captured by regulatory capital requirements are
assessed for Pillar 2 add-ons by the Group Treasurer, [PERSON_21] ([EMAIL_13]), with challenge from the Group
Chief Risk Officer.

Each regulated subsidiary maintains a board-approved local addendum documenting the local regulatory overlay, the composition of the
local risk committee and the delegated authorities of local management. Local addenda may narrow Group appetite but may never
widen it. Cross-border data flows arising from Group-level risk aggregation — which include the employee identifiers and contact details
reproduced throughout this policy — are assessed under the Data Privacy Policy before transfer.

8. Risk appetite and tolerance

Risk appetite is the aggregate level and type of risk the Group is willing to assume in pursuit of its strategy. It is the primary mechanism

and audit findings attributable to each named individual. The Committee may apply in-year adjustment, malus or clawback where risk
outcomes warrant it. A favourable financial result achieved outside appetite is not a basis for reward.

11.5 Quantitative and qualitative assessment

Where reliable data exists, risks are assessed quantitatively — through value-at-risk, expected credit loss modelling, or operational loss
distribution approaches. Where data is sparse, assessment is qualitative and structured, using expert judgement captured in facilitated
workshops with documented rationale. Qualitative assessments are not inferior, but must state their assumptions explicitly and record
who made the judgement.

11.6 Model risk

Models used to quantify risk, price instruments or determine capital are themselves a source of risk. All are registered in the Model
Inventory, tiered by materiality, independently validated before first use and revalidated at a frequency set by tier. Model limitations must
be disclosed alongside model output wherever that output informs a risk decision. Detailed requirements are in the Model Risk
Management Policy (GRP-POL-018), owned by [PERSON_49] ([EMAIL_38], [PHONE_26]).

11.7 Aggregation

Risks are aggregated to divisional and Group level using the taxonomy. Aggregation does not average ratings: the profile reports the
count and distribution of risks by band, the largest individual exposures, and any concentrations where several separately rated risks
share a common cause, control, system or third party.

16. Incident and loss event management

16.1 Reporting obligation

All incidents, near misses and loss events must be reported by the individual who becomes aware of them, through the Group Incident

18. Change, new product and third-party risk

18.1 Change risk

Change is a significant source of operational risk. All material change initiatives — system implementations, migrations, organisational
restructures, outsourcing transitions and regulatory implementation programmes — require a risk assessment at initiation, at each major
stage gate and prior to go-live. The assessment must address risks introduced by the change, risks to business-as-usual during
transition, control coverage during parallel running, and the rollback position if the change fails.

Go-live approval requires confirmation that controls have been designed, implemented and tested; that data migration has been
reconciled; that affected staff have been trained; that support arrangements are in place; and that a documented rollback plan exists.
Go-live may not proceed on the basis that controls will be implemented shortly afterwards.

18.2 New product approval

No new product, service, market or distribution channel may be launched without approval from the New Product Approval Committee,
chaired by [PERSON_86]. A material change to an existing product requires the same approval. The submission must address,
at minimum: target market, customer need and foreseeable harm; risk assessment across every applicable taxonomy category; legal,
regulatory, tax and accounting analysis; financial crime, sanctions and conduct assessment; a data privacy impact assessment where
personal data is processed; technology, resilience and third-party dependencies; operational readiness; pricing, profitability and capital
consumption; and exit and wind-down arrangements.

Every approved product is subject to post-implementation review within twelve months, assessing whether the product performed as
expected, whether customer outcomes matched those anticipated, and whether the risk assessment remains valid.

18.3 Third-party and outsourcing risk

Third-party arrangements are managed across the full lifecycle: strategy and sourcing, due diligence, contracting, onboarding, ongoing

21.4 Risk culture

Risk culture comprises the shared norms and behaviours determining how risk is actually treated, as distinct from how policy states it
should be. The Group assesses its culture annually using employee survey results, Speak Up volumes and themes, incident and issue
selfidentification rates, RCSA challenge outcomes, exception and acceptance volumes, and behavioural indicators drawn from
performance and conduct data.

A high proportion of issues identified by the business itself, rather than by audit or regulators, is treated as a positive indicator. A low
incident reporting volume is investigated rather than celebrated, on the basis that it more often indicates under-reporting than an
absence of incidents.

21.5 Speak Up

All individuals may raise concerns confidentially, and anonymously where they choose, through the Group Speak Up channel operated
independently of line management. Concerns relating to risk, control, conduct or compliance are routed to the appropriate function for

* Knowingly false or misleading attestation, assessment or report.

* Operating outside an approved limit without escalation.

* Operating under an expired risk acceptance or exception.

* Amending a limit or a rating to conceal or cure a breach.

* Retaliation against an individual who has raised a concern in good faith.
